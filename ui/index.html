Sanskrit sandhi, samāsa & verse analysis

Beta

Enter a single word or a full verse in Devanāgarī or IAST. The engine decides between sandhi, samāsa, both, or neither, and shows the padaccheda, the vigraha, and the Aṣṭādhyāyī sūtra each decision rests on.

How it decides — and what it will not claim

A split is reported only when the reconstructed members and the rule conditions are both supported. Existing spaces are source anchors, not grammatical viccheda. A verse is routed to the verse pipeline, which additionally reports the metre, the anvaya, the viśeṣya–viśeṣaṇa map, and a kāraka dependency diagram; individual words fall through to the single-pada classifier with its ML confidence.

The rule engine covers the sandhi and samāsa families exercised by the test suite, plus an ML tie-breaker trained on the bundled dataset. Śabdarūpa — the 8 × 3 subanta paradigm

Beta

Give a prātipadika and its gender to generate all twenty-four inflected padas. Every cell is tappable: opening one replays the full prakriyā step by step, with the sup affix, its anubandhas, the kāraka jurisdiction, and a four-commentary concordance.

What a cell contains

Each form carries the raw sup affix as taught in 4.1.2, the it-elision rule that strips its anubandhas, the aṅga grade (sarvanāmasthāna / bha / pada) the affix imposes, and the kāraka role the vibhakti expresses. The concordance places the Aṣṭādhyāyī step beside the Mahābhāṣya's jurisprudence, the Harināmāmṛta's devotional reading, and the Bāla-toṣaṇī exegesis for that same step.

Pada analysis & grammatical brief

Beta

Work backwards from an inflected pada to every stem, gender, vibhakti, and vacana that could have produced it. Each reading is argued as a brief: the claim, the statutes it rests on, the Mahābhāṣya dialectic, and the verdict.

Why more than one reading

Sanskrit surface forms are systematically ambiguous — रामे is both saptamī ekavacana of राम and, for other stems, a different slot entirely. Every reading whose derivation replays cleanly is listed rather than one being silently picked, and each is exportable as a standalone brief in Markdown or printable HTML.

Tiṅanta — verbal conjugation

Beta

Conjugate a dhātu across the three puruṣas and three vacanas in any of the supported lakāras, in either pada. Every cell names the sūtra that fixes its vikaraṇa and its ending.

Coverage

The bundled dhātupāṭha slice covers the common roots of the first, second, fourth, sixth, and eighth gaṇas in laṭ, laṅ, lṛṭ, loṭ, and vidhiliṅ. A root outside that slice is reported as unsupported rather than conjugated by analogy.

Chandas — syllables and metre

Beta

Paste one Sanskrit verse and this separates its metrical akṣaras, aligns the pādas by beat, then shows whether each syllable is laghu (light) or guru (heavy) — with the rule and the source behind every decision, and the metre matched against a 128-entry classical catalog.

It reads the text as recited, so spaces and hyphens do not stop a syllable. Devanāgarī and IAST are both supported; Vedic accents and word-by-word unsandhied text need separate handling.

How a verdict is graded

A pattern match alone is never called an identification. A metre is reported as identified only when the pattern matches exactly across a complete verse and its defining rule is keyed to both a root text and a commentary in two independent traditions. An exact match without that paired witness is provisional; equally-evidenced rivals are ambiguous; a single compatible pāda is fragment only. Near misses are shown as diagnostics and can never become a verdict. Both the syllable scan and the metre verdict are recomputed by an independent checker before display; a disagreement suppresses the verdict rather than showing it.

Line breaks are read as pāda boundaries; with none, daṇḍas are used, and failing that the whole text is scanned as one unit and the pādas are inferred from the metre.

Aṣṭādhyāyī — the codification

loading…

Pāṇini's grammar, rule by rule. Each sūtra here has been read against the mūla and every commentary on disk, its findings written down, and the rule itself written as a function you can run below. Nothing is claimed without a citation you can check, and where a question is still open the record says so.

A sūtra is never a self-contained statement. It is read with words carried down from earlier sūtras, under a heading that may be pages back, and against the rules it overrides or feeds. The dependency graph shows all three, and they are drawn differently because they are different things.
