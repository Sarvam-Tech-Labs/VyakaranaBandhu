Full Padacheda & Grammatical Breakdown
अत्र (atra):

Classification: Avyaya (Indeclinable).

Meaning: "Here" (referring to the Pandava army).

शूरा (śūrā):

Padacheda: शूराः (śūrāḥ)

Visarga Lopa (Dropping): The original word is śūrāḥ. Because it ends in 'āḥ' (आः) and is followed by the voiced consonant 'm' (म) in maheṣvāsā, the visarga completely drops out.

Grammar: Prathamā (1st Case), Bahuvacana (Plural). Meaning: "Heroes."

महेष्वासा (maheṣvāsā):

Padacheda: महा + इष्वासाः (mahā + iṣvāsāḥ)

Guṇa Sandhi: mahā + i = mahe.

Visarga Lopa: Again, it ends in 'āḥ' and is followed by the voiced consonant 'bh' (भ). The visarga drops.

Samas: This is a beautiful Bahuvrihi Samas (Exocentric compound).

iṣu (arrow) + ās (to throw) = iṣvāsa (bow).

mahāntaḥ iṣvāsāḥ yeṣām te (Those whose bows are great).

Grammar: Prathamā Bahuvacana. Meaning: "Mighty bowmen."

भीमार्जुनसमा (bhīmārjunasamā):

Padacheda: भीम + अर्जुन + समाः (bhīma + arjuna + samāḥ)

Dīrgha Sandhi: bhīma + a = bhīmā.

Visarga Lopa: A third time! Ends in 'āḥ', followed by 'y' (य). The visarga drops.

Samas: This contains two compounds:

Bhīmaśca arjunaśca = Bhīmārjunau (Itaretara Dvandva - Bhima and Arjuna).

Tābhyām samāḥ = Tṛtīyā Tatpurusha (Equal to them).

Grammar: Prathamā Bahuvacana. Meaning: "Equal to Bhima and Arjuna."

युधि (yudhi):

Root: yudh (battle).

Grammar: Saptamī (7th Case / Locative), Ekavacana.

Meaning: "In battle."

युयुधानो (yuyudhāno):

Padacheda: युयुधानः (yuyudhānaḥ)

Visarga Sandhi (Utva): When a word ends in 'aḥ' (अः) and is followed by a voiced consonant like 'v' (व), the 'aḥ' turns into an 'o' (ओ). Therefore, yuyudhānaḥ becomes yuyudhāno.

Grammar: Prathamā Ekavacana (Singular). This is the proper name of the warrior Satyaki.

विराटश्च (virāṭaśca):

Padacheda: विराटः + च (virāṭaḥ + ca)

Visarga Sandhi (Ścatva): When a visarga is followed by 'c' (च), it turns into the palatal 'ś' (श).

Grammar: Virāṭaḥ (Prathamā Ekavacana) - King Virata. Ca (Avyaya) - And.

द्रुपदश्च (drupadaśca):

Padacheda: द्रुपदः + च (drupadaḥ + ca)

Visarga Sandhi: Exactly the same as above. 'ḥ' + 'c' = 'ś'.

Grammar: Drupadaḥ (Prathamā Ekavacana) - King Drupada. Ca - And.

महारथः (mahārathaḥ):

Samas: Another classic Bahuvrihi Samas.

Mahān rathaḥ yasya saḥ (He whose chariot is great).

Military Context: In ancient Indian military ranks, a Maharatha wasn't just a guy with a big chariot. It was a specific rank for a commander capable of fighting 10,000 archers simultaneously!

Grammar: Prathamā Ekavacana. Meaning: "Great chariot-warrior."

The Visarga Sandhi Symphony
Notice how Panini's phonetic rules play out like music in this single verse:

Drop it (Lopa): śūrāḥ, maheṣvāsāḥ, and samāḥ all drop their visargas because the 'ā' (आ) sound is long and followed by a soft sound.

Morph it to 'O' (Utva): yuyudhānaḥ turns into yuyudhāno because the 'a' (अ) is short and followed by a soft sound.

Morph it to 'Ś' (Ścatva): virāṭaḥ and drupadaḥ turn into ś (श्) because they hit the hard, palatal sound 'c' (च).

Three totally different rules for the exact same symbol (ः), all happening in one line of poetry!
